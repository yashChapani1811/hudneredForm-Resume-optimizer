7 Questions You Must Ask in Every Interview

The questions you ask tell employers how you think. They also help you decide whether the role is worth your energy.

March 8, 20248 min readHunderedform Team

A strong interview is not only about giving good answers. It is also about asking questions that reveal how the team works, what the role truly requires, and whether the company is set up to help you succeed.

Most candidates wait until the final five minutes and ask something safe like "What is the culture like?" The problem is that broad questions usually get broad answers. Better questions create useful detail. They show that you think beyond the job title and that you care about outcomes, expectations, and fit.

1. What would success look like in the first 90 days?

This question turns a vague job description into a practical performance conversation. Listen for specific projects, metrics, relationships, and priorities. If the interviewer cannot explain success, the role may not be well defined.

What to listen for: a clear onboarding path, realistic expectations, and one or two priorities that matter more than everything else.

2. What problem made this role necessary?

Every open role exists because something needs to change. Maybe the team is growing, customers need more support, reporting is messy, revenue targets increased, or a previous employee left. The answer tells you the real business need behind the vacancy.

3. How does the team make decisions?

This reveals more about culture than asking directly about culture. A healthy team can explain who owns decisions, how disagreement is handled, and how information moves. A vague answer may signal confusion or too many approval layers.

4. What are the biggest challenges someone will face in this role?

This is where the conversation becomes honest. Every role has friction. You want to know whether the challenge is normal and solvable, such as learning a complex product, or structural, such as unclear ownership, constant emergencies, or impossible workload.

5. How will my performance be measured?

Clear metrics create clear expectations. Depending on the role, listen for revenue targets, customer outcomes, delivery timelines, quality standards, stakeholder satisfaction, or operational improvements. If success is subjective, ask how feedback is given.

6. What do top performers here do differently?

This question helps you understand the unwritten rules. The answer may reveal whether the company values speed, documentation, customer empathy, technical depth, ownership, collaboration, or executive communication.

7. Is there anything in my background you would like me to clarify?

This gives you a chance to address doubt before the interview ends. It is confident without being pushy. If they mention a gap, tool, or experience area, respond with a specific example and connect it back to the role.

Questions to avoid asking too early

  • Anything that is answered clearly on the company website.
  • Questions focused only on vacation, remote work, or promotion before fit is established.
  • Overly broad questions like "How is the culture?" without a specific angle.
  • Questions that sound like you have not read the job description.

Frequently asked question

How many questions should I ask?

Prepare 6-8, but expect to ask 3-5. Some questions will be answered naturally during the conversation, and that is a good thing.
