How to Write a Resume That Passes ATS Scanners

An ATS-friendly resume is readable, specific, and matched to the role. This guide shows the practical steps before you upload your next application.

March 10, 20246 min readHunderedform Team

An ATS-friendly resume is not a stripped-down, robotic document. It is a clear resume that helps software understand your experience and helps a recruiter quickly see why you fit the role.

Most job seekers lose interviews because their resume makes the reader work too hard. The job title is unclear, the skills are scattered, the bullet points sound like a job description, and the best evidence is buried near the bottom. ATS software magnifies those problems because it depends on structure and language.

The goal: write a resume that is easy to parse, easy to scan, and strongly aligned with one target role.

Start with the job description, not a blank page

Before editing your resume, copy the job description into a document and highlight the repeated skills, tools, responsibilities, and outcomes. If the role mentions "stakeholder management," "SQL dashboards," "sales pipeline," or "compliance reporting," those phrases tell you what the employer expects to see.

This does not mean stuffing keywords everywhere. It means using the employer's language when it honestly describes your experience. A recruiter should be able to compare the job post and your resume and immediately see the connection.

Use standard sections ATS tools understand

Keep the structure simple: Summary, Skills, Experience, Education, Certifications, and Projects if needed. Creative section names can look interesting, but they often make parsing weaker. ATS tools are built around predictable resume patterns.

  • Use "Experience" instead of "Where I Made an Impact."
  • Use "Skills" instead of "Toolbox."
  • Use consistent date formats like "Jan 2022 - Mar 2024."
  • Keep important information out of headers, footers, and images.

Write a focused professional summary

Your summary should not say you are motivated, passionate, or hardworking. It should answer three questions: what role you do, what you are strongest at, and what kind of result you create.

Weak:

Hardworking professional with strong communication skills seeking a challenging role.

Stronger:

Operations analyst with 4 years of experience improving reporting workflows, building Excel and SQL dashboards, and reducing manual reconciliation time across finance teams.

Turn responsibilities into proof

ATS scanners can identify keywords, but recruiters decide whether your experience feels credible. That credibility comes from outcomes. A strong bullet point includes the action, scope, tool, and result.

Before:

Responsible for weekly reports and team coordination.

After:

Built weekly KPI reports in Excel and SQL for 12 regional managers, reducing manual update time by 6 hours per week and improving visibility into delayed orders.

Place keywords where they naturally belong

Use a dedicated skills section for tools and technical capabilities, then reinforce the most important skills inside your experience bullets. If "Salesforce" appears only in a skills list but never in your work history, it feels thin. If it appears in a bullet with context, it feels real.

Keep formatting clean and boring in the best way

Use a single-column layout for online applications, avoid text boxes, avoid scanned PDFs, and keep icons minimal. Decorative design should never block readability. The best ATS resume looks clean enough for software and polished enough for a person.

Final ATS resume checklist

  • One target role is clear within the first 10 seconds.
  • Top job-description keywords appear naturally.
  • Every recent role has measurable bullet points.
  • Dates, titles, and company names are consistent.
  • The file is text-based PDF or DOCX, not a scanned image.
  • The resume ends with no generic filler or outdated skills.

Frequently asked question

Should I make a separate resume for every job?

You do not need to rewrite everything. Build one strong base resume for each target role, then adjust the summary, skills order, and 3-5 bullet points for each application.
